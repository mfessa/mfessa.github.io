About Me

Researcher at the intersection of sustainable energy and computational intelligence

I am a postdoctoral researcher specializing in multiscale kinetic modelling and machine learning for thermochemical conversion of agricultural biomass. My work focuses on developing digital twins — self-learning virtual replicas of pyrolysis reactors — that combine first-principles physics with data-driven intelligence.

My research spans the full spectrum of biomass pyrolysis science: from molecular-level reaction kinetics (Broido–Shafizadeh schemes), through intraparticle heat and mass transport, up to reactor-scale hydrodynamics. I leverage modern ML techniques — Physics-Informed Neural Networks, Deep Operator Networks, and Gaussian Processes — to create surrogates that are both fast enough for real-time control and accurate enough for industrial deployment.
